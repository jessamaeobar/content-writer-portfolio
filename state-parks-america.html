10 State Parks That Showcase America's Remarkable Landscapes

America's state parks preserve some of the country's most memorable landscapes, from desert formations and volcanic cliffs to waterfalls, deep gorges, and vast forests. These ten parks stand out for the variety of scenery and experiences they offer travelers.

1. Custer State Park, South Dakota

Spread across the Black Hills, Custer State Park combines granite formations, rolling grasslands, forests, and abundant wildlife. The park is especially known for its large bison herd, scenic drives, and dramatic routes such as Needles Highway.

Wildlife viewing is one of the park's biggest draws, but the landscape itself is reason enough to visit. Curving roads, rocky spires, and open prairie create a distinctly western setting within the Black Hills.

2. Silver Falls State Park, Oregon

Silver Falls State Park is home to one of Oregon's best-known waterfall trails. The Trail of Ten Falls follows a route through a forested canyon and passes ten waterfalls, including several that visitors can walk behind.

The combination of dense forest, rushing water, and basalt cliffs gives the park a cinematic quality. It is the kind of place where the landscape keeps changing as the trail moves through the canyon.

3. Valley of Fire State Park, Nevada

Just outside Las Vegas, Valley of Fire looks dramatically different from the city. Its red Aztec sandstone formations were shaped over millions of years, producing arches, ridges, narrow passages, and other unusual forms.

The park also preserves evidence of human history, including petroglyphs left by ancient peoples. Short hikes and scenic drives make it accessible while still revealing the scale and variety of the desert landscape.

4. Letchworth State Park, New York

Known as the “Grand Canyon of the East,” Letchworth State Park follows the Genesee River through a deep gorge carved into the landscape of western New York. Three major waterfalls are among its defining features.

The park is particularly striking in autumn, when the surrounding forests add another layer of color to the gorge. Hiking trails, overlooks, and picnic areas provide different ways to take in the scenery.

5. Smith Rock State Park, Oregon

Smith Rock rises above the Crooked River in central Oregon, where towering cliffs and volcanic formations create one of the state's most recognizable landscapes.

The park is famous among climbers and is widely associated with the development of modern sport climbing in the United States. Hikers can also explore trails along the canyon and take in views that extend across Oregon's high desert.

6. Fall Creek Falls State Park, Tennessee

Fall Creek Falls State Park protects a rugged landscape of waterfalls, gorges, forests, lakes, and streams in eastern Tennessee. Its namesake waterfall is one of the park's most prominent features.

The park offers a wide variety of outdoor activities, from hiking and paddling to fishing and camping. Its mix of water and forest makes it especially appealing to travelers who want several kinds of scenery in one destination.

7. Itasca State Park, Minnesota

Itasca State Park is best known as the home of the headwaters of the Mississippi River. Established in 1891, it is also Minnesota's oldest state park.

The park's landscape includes forests, wetlands, lakes, and more than 32,000 acres of protected land. Visitors can walk across the Mississippi at its beginning, then continue exploring the woods and waterways that surround Lake Itasca.

8. Dead Horse Point State Park, Utah

Dead Horse Point State Park overlooks a sweeping bend of the Colorado River, with layers of canyon country stretching across the horizon. Its high desert scenery is defined by red rock, cliffs, and immense open views.

The park is especially striking at sunrise and sunset, when changing light brings out the contours of the surrounding canyonlands. Several hiking and mountain-biking trails provide closer views of the plateau and cliffs.

9. Baxter State Park, Maine

Baxter State Park offers a very different experience from the desert parks of the American West. Covering more than 200,000 acres in northern Maine, it protects rugged mountains, lakes, forests, and remote backcountry.

Mount Katahdin is the park's defining landmark and the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail. The area's scale and emphasis on wilderness give Baxter a quieter, more rugged character than many heavily developed parks.

10. Devil's Den State Park, Arkansas

Located in the Lee Creek Valley of northwest Arkansas, Devil's Den State Park combines forested hills, rock formations, caves, and waterways within the Ozark landscape.

The park is also notable for its collection of structures built by the Civilian Conservation Corps during the 1930s. Trails, cabins, and historic stonework allow visitors to experience both the area's natural scenery and an important period of American conservation history.

Landscapes Worth Exploring

These parks show just how varied the American landscape can be. Granite spires rise from the Black Hills, waterfalls cut through Oregon forests, red sandstone glows in Nevada, and rivers carve deep gorges across New York and Utah. In places such as Itasca and Baxter, forests and waterways create a quieter kind of drama.

Together, they offer more than scenic views. They show how geology, wildlife, history, and recreation can intersect in places protected for generations to experience.
