INDEPENDENT PR PORTFOLIO EXERCISE

Don't Take “Works Locally” on Trust

A hypothetical consumer-tech PR strategy for CES 2027: research the context, find the editorial question, and turn it into a testable media story.

Consumer Technology Story Strategy Media Research CES 2027
Important disclosure

This is an independent hypothetical exercise. Calderline Home and its product are fictional. No prototype has been tested, no journalist or creator has been contacted, and no campaign has been executed. Proposed product features are scenario assumptions—not verified claims. The sources below support industry context, not the fictional product.

1. The brief: make the claim testable

Fictional company

Calderline Home

A proposed startup for people who want basic household alerts without cameras or microphones.

Fictional product

Calderline Kit

A hypothetical kit with door/window contact sensors, a water-leak sensor, a temperature sensor, and a local hub.

In this scenario, the kit's local-only configuration would let users view supported events on a local dashboard without an external internet connection. Remote alerts would not be available in that configuration. A plain-language data map would describe what each sensor records and how information is handled.

Every capability would need to be verified against a working prototype and technical documentation before outreach. Local control is not a new invention, and neither “private” nor “secure” should be used as a blanket claim.

The editorial question
When a smart-home system loses its external internet connection, what still works—and what evidence tells us where its data goes?

The first part can be tested with a controlled functionality check. Data handling requires separate technical evidence; an offline test alone cannot prove privacy or security.

2. Research: what the evidence supports

Users' privacy perceptions differ by device category

A NIST study published in December 2025 surveyed 401 U.S. smart-home users across five categories: lighting, security devices, sensors, thermostats, and voice assistants. In that sample, participants viewed voice assistants as the most problematic category and expressed greater confidence in security devices and thermostats.

Strategic use: The study supports treating device category as relevant context. It does not establish current demand for this fictional product or prove that U.S. perceptions reflect European audiences.

Research limitation: Although published in December 2025, the survey data were collected in February 2022. The sample was not designed to be fully representative of the U.S. population. These findings provide background context, not proof of current market demand.

Local connectivity is established

The Connectivity Standards Alliance's Matter FAQ explains that Matter can enable local connections, while manufacturers may retain cloud connectivity for remote control and other functions. The behavior depends on the device and setup.

Strategic use: Don't pitch local control alone as novel. The potential angle is the product's specific behavior, and whether a journalist can check that behavior against clear documentation.

Security claims require evidence

The FTC's connected-device guidance discusses secure design, access controls, data management, and limiting collection and retention to what is needed. A data map is useful documentation, but it is not a security certification.

What existing coverage tells us

A preliminary scan shows that local control, Matter, and smart-home privacy have already received editorial attention. For example, The Verge covered Google's local Matter control in January 2025; TechRadar examined smart-home privacy risks in May 2026; and WIRED explained the Matter standard in May 2025.

Implication: “Local control is new” would be a weak pitch. A more defensible approach is to offer a transparent, repeatable product test and be explicit about what it can—and cannot—prove. These articles are research examples, not confirmed targets or evidence of interest.

Research boundary

These sources establish context. They do not validate Calderline Home, show that consumers want it, or predict media interest. Those questions would need further research and a real, testable product.

3. Three angles considered

Angle A · Launch

A new monitoring kit at CES

Audience value: Launch-calendar relevance.

Evidence needed: Confirmed specs, timing, availability, imagery.

Weakness: An announcement alone offers little distinction.

Angle B · Use case

Home alerts without cameras or microphones

Audience value: A tangible conversation about sensor-based monitoring.

Evidence needed: Working demo and clear boundaries of what sensors detect.

Weakness: Could feel basic without a real user benefit.

Angle C · Selected

Put the local-control claim to a test

Audience value: Journalists can examine observed behavior instead of repeating a broad claim.

Evidence needed: Prototype, test protocol, expected-behavior checklist, data-flow documentation.

Weakness: Local control is established; the value must come from the evidence.

I would lead with Angle C. The demonstration is a method, not news by itself. It becomes a useful story only if the product can be tested credibly and the results help an audience understand a real choice. If the test produces no meaningful finding, the angle should change—not be exaggerated.

4. The test: separate functionality from data handling

Check Method What it can establish What it cannot establish alone
Offline functionality Keep the local network running, disconnect external internet access, trigger each sensor with a repeatable procedure, and record the dashboard response. Which tested functions remain available under the defined conditions. That the product is secure, transmits no data in other conditions, or offers a unique capability.
Observed network activity Use a suitable technical method to observe communications during normal use and compare them with documented data flows. Which communications were observed within the test method's limits. That every possible data flow has been ruled out. Encryption may limit what inspection can reveal.
Documentation match Compare observations with specifications and stated data collection, storage, retention, or deletion practices. Whether the documentation matches observed behavior in the tested setup. A complete security certification or a guarantee about future versions.

A technically qualified reviewer would need to validate the procedure and interpret the evidence. Any discrepancy between the prototype and its documentation should be addressed before the affected claim is pitched.

5. Strategy and media qualification

Objective: Generate qualified consideration for a hypothetical CES 2027 demonstration among relevant U.S. and European consumer-tech journalists and creators. Useful early signals include substantive questions, requests for technical information, demonstration bookings, or consideration of a hands-on review—not just emails sent.

Consumer-tech reviewers

Need: Testable features, specifications, real-world limitations.

Angle: Which functions remain available under a defined test?

Connected-device reporters

Need: Technical context and clear product differences.

Angle: What depends on the local network and what needs an external service?

Privacy / digital-living writers

Need: Evidence about data collection, storage, transmission, and retention.

Angle: Does the data explanation match observed behavior?

Technology creators

Need: A visual demonstration and time to test independently.

Angle: Show normal operation, run the test, explain the trade-offs.

For a real list, I would review recent bylines, product reviews, and creator videos; record the beat, audience, geography, relevant work, contact source, and best-fit angle; and verify contact details before outreach. The official CES exhibitor PR guidance likewise recommends researching journalist interests, using product-category filters, personalizing short emails, and not blasting the full list.

The facts would remain consistent across markets. Outreach to U.S. reviewers could focus on product behavior and practical evaluation. For U.K. and other European outlets, I would first confirm availability, relevant compatibility, support, and which claims apply in that market rather than assume one English-language message fits all of Europe. Paid creator activity, if any, would be reported separately from earned editorial coverage.

6. Sample journalist pitch

Recipient: [Consumer-technology reporter preparing CES coverage]

Subject: CES 2027: What happens when you disconnect a smart-home system?

Hi [First Name],

“Works locally” can mean different things depending on the device. A useful test is to disconnect the external internet connection and see which functions remain available—and which stop working.

Calderline Home is a fictional product concept for this portfolio exercise. Its proposed CES demonstration would let a reviewer trigger several household sensors, keep the local network running while disconnecting the internet, and compare the observed behavior with a documented test protocol.

A separate technical walkthrough would examine the documented data flows and explain the limits of what network observations can establish. The aim isn't to claim local control is new; it's to make the product's behavior open to examination.

Would a demonstration built around those tests be relevant to your CES coverage?

Best,
[Your Name]

Why this pitch: It leads with a concrete question, explains the demonstration quickly, acknowledges the existing landscape, and offers a specific next step. A real pitch would require a tested prototype, verified claims, and personalization to the recipient's recent work.

7. Adapt the opening; keep the facts

Consumer-tech reviewer

“A smart-home device may work perfectly while the internet is connected. The more revealing test is what happens when that connection disappears. Calderline Home's proposed CES demonstration would let you test local functions and compare the result with the product's documented specifications.”

Why: It prioritizes repeatable testing and product limitations.

Privacy / digital-living writer

“A privacy explanation is useful only if people can understand what a device collects and whether that explanation matches its behavior. Calderline Home's proposed demonstration would pair an offline-functionality check with a walkthrough of its documented data flows.”

Why: It emphasizes transparency without treating offline operation as proof of privacy.

Follow-up email

Subject: Re: CES 2027: What happens when you disconnect a smart-home system?

Hi [First Name],

One detail that may help you assess the idea: the proposed briefing would separate the offline-functionality test from the examination of network communications. That makes clear both what the system does and what the tests cannot establish about data handling.

I can share the proposed test outline and documentation checklist if they would help you decide whether the subject fits your CES plans.

Best,
[Your Name]

In a real campaign, timing would follow agency guidance and the recipient's preferences. Outreach would stop if the recipient declined or asked not to be contacted.

8. CES plan and reporting

CES 2027 is scheduled for January 6–9, 2027, in Las Vegas. CES lists Media Days for January 4–5, Unveiled Las Vegas on January 4, and access to the exhibitor media list through the Exhibitor Dashboard from December 1. See the official show dates, media opportunities, and exhibitor PR guidance. The proposed plan below is not an official deadline calendar.

  • Research and validate Confirm the claims, test procedure, and required evidence with a qualified technical reviewer.
  • Qualify media and prepare assets Build a justified list and prepare verified specs, test outline, data-flow documents, images, and spokesperson answers.
  • Targeted outreach Send concise, personalized pitches; track responses, questions, and next steps; book demonstrations only when confirmed.
  • Event and post-event Support scheduled briefings, verify published coverage, record message accuracy, and capture recurring questions.
Measure Reporting purpose
Qualified contacts and personalized pitches Track audience fit and completed outreach activity.
Meaningful replies and requests Assess whether the angle prompts substantive engagement or an actionable next step.
Published coverage and message accuracy Verify outcomes and check whether product claims and limits are represented correctly.
Audience relevance and referral traffic, where trackable Assess whether coverage reached the intended audience and generated measurable visits.

The report would distinguish activity from outcomes, and earned coverage from sponsored content. Verified coverage and recurring journalist questions could inform future messaging and approved sales or marketing assets. No campaign results are claimed for this exercise.

9. What this exercise demonstrates

This case study begins with public research, checks the competitive context, compares three story angles, and selects one that can be investigated with evidence. It then translates that decision into media qualification, an audience-specific pitch, a respectful follow-up, and reporting logic.

The principle behind the work

A product announcement tells people what a company wants them to know. A strong media angle gives a journalist a question worth investigating, a credible way to investigate it, and evidence to judge what the answer means.
