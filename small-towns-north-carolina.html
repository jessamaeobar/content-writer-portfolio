10 Small Towns in North Carolina That Are Worth the Detour

North Carolina has no shortage of well-known destinations, but some of its most interesting places are found beyond the usual stops. From coastal communities shaped by maritime history to mountain towns surrounded by waterfalls and forest, these small towns offer a closer look at the state's history, landscapes, and local character.

1. Beaufort

Set along North Carolina's Crystal Coast, Beaufort combines maritime history with a walkable waterfront and a well-preserved historic district. The town's connection to the sea is especially evident at the North Carolina Maritime Museum, where exhibits explore the state's coastal heritage and the history of shipwrecks, including the remains of Blackbeard's flagship, Queen Anne's Revenge.

Visitors can stroll along the waterfront, browse local shops and galleries, or explore nearby Fort Macon State Park. Beaufort is a good choice for travelers who want coastal scenery without giving up a strong sense of history.

2. Hillsborough

Hillsborough has the kind of historic setting that rewards travelers who slow down and explore on foot. Founded in the eighteenth century along the Eno River, the town played a role in North Carolina's colonial and Revolutionary-era history.

Its present-day appeal extends beyond historic buildings. Hillsborough has become known for its arts scene, independent shops, and riverfront trails. The Occoneechee Speedway Trail adds another unusual piece of history: the site was once home to a NASCAR speedway and is now a public trail where visitors can walk through the remnants of the historic track.

3. Edenton

On the Albemarle Sound, Edenton offers a glimpse into North Carolina's colonial past. The town became North Carolina's first colonial capital in 1722 and remained an important political center during the colony's early history.

Edenton is also associated with the Edenton Tea Party of 1774, an organized act of resistance to British taxation led by local women. Today, visitors can explore historic homes and sites such as the Barker House while enjoying the town's waterfront setting and quiet streets.

4. Blowing Rock

Blowing Rock sits in North Carolina's High Country near the Blue Ridge Parkway, where mountain scenery is part of everyday life. The town takes its name from the famous Blowing Rock, a cliff overlooking the Johns River Gorge.

Its location also makes it a convenient base for exploring nearby mountain attractions, including Grandfather Mountain and sections of the Blue Ridge Parkway. The combination of elevation, scenery, and a compact downtown gives Blowing Rock an appeal that goes beyond its famous landmark.

5. Black Mountain

Just east of Asheville, Black Mountain is surrounded by the Blue Ridge Mountains and has developed a reputation for its independent shops, galleries, restaurants, and outdoor access.

The town's location makes it easy to combine a downtown visit with time outdoors. Nearby trails and mountain scenery provide opportunities for hiking and sightseeing, while the compact center makes Black Mountain especially easy to explore at a relaxed pace.

6. Brevard

Brevard is one of the best places in North Carolina for travelers drawn to waterfalls and forest landscapes. Located near Pisgah National Forest and DuPont State Recreational Forest, the area is associated with more than 250 waterfalls.

Looking Glass Falls is one of the most recognizable, while Sliding Rock offers a different kind of experience: a natural rock waterslide formed by a mountain stream. For visitors who enjoy hiking, swimming holes, and scenic drives, Brevard makes a strong base for exploring western North Carolina.

7. Bryson City

Bryson City lies near the Great Smoky Mountains and provides easy access to one of the region's most celebrated mountain landscapes. The town is also home to the Great Smoky Mountains Railroad, which offers scenic train excursions through the surrounding mountains and valleys.

Beyond the railroad, Bryson City is a gateway to outdoor recreation, with opportunities for hiking, fishing, rafting, and exploring the nearby national park. Its location gives the town a distinctly Appalachian character while keeping major natural attractions within easy reach.

8. Bath

Bath is one of North Carolina's oldest historic communities and was the state's first town, chartered in 1705. Its small size makes its history easy to appreciate at a leisurely pace.

The town is also closely associated with the pirate Blackbeard, who lived in the area for a time. Historic Bath offers preserved structures and sites that help tell the story of North Carolina's early coastal settlements, while nearby waterways reinforce the town's maritime character.

9. New Bern

New Bern has an unusually rich history for a small North Carolina city. It became North Carolina's first permanent capital in the eighteenth century, and Tryon Palace later served as the seat of colonial government.

The town is also famous as the birthplace of Pepsi-Cola. Pharmacist Caleb Bradham developed the drink in New Bern in the 1890s, and visitors can learn more about that history at the Birthplace of Pepsi-Cola. With the Neuse and Trent Rivers surrounding the city, New Bern also offers waterfront scenery alongside its historic attractions.

10. Sylva

Sylva sits in western North Carolina's mountains and serves as the county seat of Jackson County. One of its most recognizable landmarks is the historic Jackson County Courthouse, which rises above downtown.

The town has also found an unexpected place in film history. Sylva and nearby locations were used in the filming of Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri, giving parts of the downtown area an additional layer of pop-culture interest.

Beyond the courthouse and movie connections, Sylva is a practical base for exploring the surrounding mountains, rivers, and waterfalls of western North Carolina.

A Different Side of North Carolina

North Carolina's smaller towns show how varied the state can be. Along the coast, places such as Beaufort, Edenton, and Bath connect visitors with maritime and colonial history. In the mountains, Blowing Rock, Brevard, Bryson City, Black Mountain, and Sylva provide access to forests, rivers, waterfalls, and Blue Ridge scenery. Hillsborough and New Bern add another dimension through their political, cultural, and commercial histories.

For travelers willing to venture beyond the state's best-known destinations, these towns offer a more intimate way to experience North Carolina—one historic street, mountain trail, or waterfront at a time.
