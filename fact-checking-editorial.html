Original AI Draft

Shopping Online Safely in Australia

Online shopping is increasingly popular in Australia, but consumers should be careful when buying from unfamiliar websites.

In 2025, Australians lost more than $260 million to online shopping scams, making them the country's most financially damaging scam category. More than 12,000 Australians lost money to shopping scams during the year.

Most shopping scams happen through fake websites, so consumers should never trust a website simply because it looks professional.

Australian Consumer Law protects online shoppers, and businesses must provide a refund whenever a customer is unhappy with a purchase. This applies even when a customer simply changes their mind.

Fact-Check

Draft claim Source check Editorial decision
“Online shopping is increasingly popular in Australia.” Unsupported. The draft provides no source for this broad trend claim. Removed
“Australians lost more than $260 million to online shopping scams.” Incorrect. The ACCC's $259.5 million figure referred to total scam losses reported to Scamwatch between January and September 2025, not shopping scams. Full-year reported shopping-scam losses were $10.8 million. Corrected
“Shopping scams were the country's most financially damaging scam category.” Misleading. Shopping scams had the highest number of reports involving a financial loss, but investment scams recorded the highest total reported losses in 2025. Rewritten
“More than 12,000 Australians lost money to shopping scams.” Supported. The ACCC reported 12,248 Australians who reported a loss to shopping scams in 2025. Kept
“Most shopping scams happen through fake websites.” Overstated. The ACCC identifies online content—including fake websites, advertisements, social media and mobile apps—as common methods used by scammers, but the source does not establish that fake websites account for most shopping scams specifically. Rewritten
“Businesses must provide a refund whenever a customer is unhappy with a purchase.” Incorrect. Consumer guarantees provide remedies when specific guarantees are not met. A business does not generally have to provide a refund when a customer simply changes their mind. Corrected

Revised Version

Shopping Online Safely in Australia

In 2025, 12,248 Australians reported losing money to shopping scams, with total reported losses of $10.8 million. More Australians reported financial losses from shopping scams than from any other scam type.

Scammers use a range of online channels to reach potential victims, including fake websites, advertisements, social media and mobile apps.

Australian Consumer Law gives consumers certain protections when a product or service does not meet a consumer guarantee. However, those guarantees do not generally entitle a customer to a refund simply because they changed their mind about a purchase.

Editorial Decisions

What I Checked

  • Whether numerical claims matched the source, category, and reporting period.
  • Whether terms such as “most” and “highest” were supported by the evidence.
  • Whether the draft distinguished between the number of people reporting losses and the total dollar value of losses.
  • Whether broad claims were supported by the cited evidence.

What I Changed

  • Corrected the $260 million figure and its scope.
  • Distinguished shopping scams by number of reports involving a loss from scam categories by total financial loss.
  • Removed an unsupported trend statement.
  • Reworked an overgeneralised claim about fake websites.
  • Corrected the statement about refunds and change-of-mind purchases.
  • Cut repetition and tightened the article without changing its purpose.
